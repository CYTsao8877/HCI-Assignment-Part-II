Elevator Fitts' Law Experiment

Effects of button size and spacing on floor-selection performance

Study design

A passenger selects floors 1–6 on a fixed, symmetrical elevator panel. Floors 1–3 are placed from top to bottom in the left column; floors 4–6 are placed from top to bottom in the right column.

Research question: How do elevator-button size and spacing affect movement time?

Fixed clicking sequence1 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 3 → 2 → 1The first click on Floor 1 is for positioning. The following six movements are recorded.

The arrangement stays fixed. Only button diameter and the equal horizontal/vertical center-to-center spacing change between blocks. Every block starts with an unmeasured positioning click on Floor 1, followed by six equal-distance movements: 1→4→5→6→3→2→1. Each floor is selected exactly once as a measured target. The six movement times are averaged to produce one data point per condition, giving nine points for the regression.

Scenario, innovation, and application

Scenario: A passenger must quickly select a destination floor using an elevator control panel.

Innovation: The experiment uses a recognizable six-floor panel, a controlled movement path, and systematic variations in button size and spacing.

Application: Results can inform compact, efficient, and accessible elevator-panel layouts.
